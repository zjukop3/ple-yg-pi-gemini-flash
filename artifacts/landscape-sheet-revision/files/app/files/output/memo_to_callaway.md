*   **Rainbow Boulevard (Entry Frontage, East Side):** 420 LF — 8 trees shown

Under Clark County Code § 30.64.030(k) and Clark County Guidance Memorandum CP-PW-GM-2025-04 (*Shade and Canopy Guidance for New Residential Development*), streetscape palettes falling into the medium canopy class (which includes Desert Willow [*Chilopsis linearis*], Desert Museum Palo Verde [*Parkinsonia x 'Desert Museum'*], and Thornless Mesquite [*Prosopis 'Phoenix'*]) require **one (1) tree per twenty (20) linear feet of frontage**.

$$\text{Required Trees} = \frac{4,160 \text{ LF}}{20 \text{ LF/tree}} = 208 \text{ trees}$$

The current submittal provides only 58 street trees—an average spacing of 71.7 feet on center—leaving a deficit of **150 street trees** (a 258% increase in street tree density).

#### 1.3 Alternative Standards Mechanism under § 30.64.050 vs. Tree Redline
If the physical geometry of the streetscape, driveways, and utility corridors cannot mathematically accommodate 208 medium canopy trees at rigid 20-foot centers, Clark County Code provides an established relief valve under **Title 30, Chapter 30.64, sec. 30.64.050 (Alternative Standards)**, as detailed in Guidance Memorandum CP-PW-GM-2025-04 (Section 4):
*   An applicant may submit a written Alternative Standards request proposing alternative tree spacing or the substitution of higher-canopy species where site conditions warrant (such as subsurface utility corridors, sight-distance triangles, or narrow frontage geometry).
*   However, relief is contingent upon demonstrating that the project achieves the County’s **30% shade coverage performance target** (aggregate 15-year canopy area divided by streetscape area) via a formal **Shade Coverage Exhibit**.
*   **Crucial Caveat:** Approval of an Alternative Standards proposal is discretionary with Comprehensive Planning and requires written justification. It cannot be resolved by an informal CAD redline.

### PART 2: THE TURF ISSUE (COMMENTS L-2, L-3, L-5) & COUNSEL’S MEMO

#### 2.1 The Fatal Flaw in Counsel’s Exemption Position
In your email, you directed Sundial to treat Comment L-2 as a clerical redline, adopt Adrienne Vogel’s October 20, 2026 legal memorandum, and move on. **Adopting this approach will result in immediate rejection by Clark County and the SNWA.**

Adrienne Vogel argues that Nevada AB 356 (2021) and the regional nonfunctional turf prohibition apply only to "non-single-family residential properties," and therefore Phase 3 is exempt because it is zoned R-2 single-family. This analysis is legally inaccurate under adopted administrative rules:
1.  **SNWA Compliance Bulletin WRC-2024-07 (Section 1, Pages 1–2):**
    > *"The single-family residential carve-out is lot-based, not project-based. The following areas are outside the carve-out and remain fully subject to the prohibition even where they lie within a subdivision the zoning of which is single-family residential: HOA-owned or HOA-managed common-area parcels of any description, including but not limited to park parcels, tot lots, greenbelts, drainage tracts, entry features, monument islands, and landscape maintenance strips."*
2.  **Clark County Code Title 30 § 30.64.030(j) & Tentative Map Condition 27:**

    *   Tentative Map Condition 20 explicitly clarifies that the tentative map approval does *not* vest landscape or water conservation standards against subsequently enacted regulations.

#### 2.2 Callaway’s Value-Engineering Cut Created Nonfunctional Turf
Under SNWA Bulletin WRC-2024-07 (Section 2), turf in an HOA common-area park is recognized as functional **only** if it qualifies under one of two narrow definitions:
1.  **Playground Turf:** Defined as *"turf located within a designated play area containing playground amenities (such as swing sets, slides, climbing structures, or play equipment) designed and installed for children’s use, where the turf serves as the safety surface or immediate active play area under and around the equipment."*
2.  **Athletic Field Turf:** Regulated sports fields meeting strict size and programming minimums.

**What Happened in Phase 3:** In July 2026, Callaway value-engineered out all playground structures (slides, swings, climbing apparatus) to reduce capital costs. On Sheet L-3 as submitted, Parcel A consists solely of an 18,400 sq. ft. passive open lawn surrounded by a walking path and four bench nodes. 
*   Because there are **zero playground amenities**, this 18,400 sq. ft. turf area is legally classified as **NONFUNCTIONAL TURF**.
*   Under Nevada law and SNWA rules, installation of new nonfunctional turf is prohibited, and existing nonfunctional turf faces a mandatory conversion deadline of **December 31, 2026**.
*   Dale Yeoman’s Comment L-2 specifically caught this violation:
    > *"Provide the turf classification analysis and supporting tabulation required by the applicable code standard and SNWA guidance for turf areas within the common-area park. Include area, category, and configuration for each turf hydrozone."*

If Sundial simply cites counsel’s memo in the comment response matrix, Public Works Development Review and Comprehensive Planning will issue a non-compliance determination, stopping the plan check dead in its tracks.
